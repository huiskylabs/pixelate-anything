# ink-wash

## Essence

An ink painting rebuilt in pixels: five to seven ink values on warm paper, mist as bare paper, brush clusters that taper and break, one small red accent (a seal, a lantern). Space is what is left unpainted.

## What it is not

- Not `contre-jour`: no light layer, no navy night, no glow; the paper is the light.
- Not `illustrated`: no carved stone ramps or haze colours, only ink values.
- Not a filter over a photo or a blurred watercolour: every stroke is a hand-placed pixel cluster.

## Pitfalls

- Filling the sky: bare paper must stay at least 40% of the frame.
- Outlines: ink-wash has strokes, not contours; an outlined rock reads as a cartoon.
- Even strokes: a stroke that keeps one width looks like vector art; taper it and break it with dry-brush gaps.
- Colour creep: one red accent, nothing else saturated.

## Variation space

Free in every piece, so two pieces in this style should not look alike:

- Subject, season, time of day, the figure and the one moving thing.
- Paper warmth (cream to grey), which ink tone dominates, where the empty paper sits.
- The red accent (a seal, a lantern, a robe) and where it sits; any short calligraphy as original marks, never real text copied from a work.
- Music: sparse and spacious, instruments free.
